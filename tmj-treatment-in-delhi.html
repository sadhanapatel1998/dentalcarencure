What is TMJ disorder?

Temporomandibular Joint (TMJ) is the term given to the joint in your skull through which your lower jaw moves to allow you to talk, chew, and even yawn. TMJ is involved in various other motions like speaking and yawning. If this joint gets tired, irritated, or is out of alignment, you experience TMJ disorder.

TMJ disorder can also be caused by:

  • Bruxism
  • Jaw injury
  • Arthritis affecting the jaw joint
  • Muscle tension due to stress
  • Bite misalignment
  • Jaw clenching

The problem might occur either on one side or on both sides of the jaw. This disease can cause pain, locked joints, and difficulty moving the jaw. Tension headaches, facial pain, ear problems, clicks and even migraines are very frequent.

  • Discomfort and tightness in the jaw area

  • Sounds like clicking, popping, and grinding while moving the jaw

Common Questions About TMJ Disorders and Jaw Pain

Can TMJ disorders lead to migraine?

Yes. It is possible because TMJ disorders might result in stress on the jaw muscle structure and thereby cause frequent headaches and migraines.

How can I tell if I need TMJ treatment?

Jaw pain, clicking sound, headache, facial pain, problems with chewing, inability to open your mouth wide – these symptoms signal the necessity of TMJ examination.

Is the procedure painful?

Most treatment methods used for dealing with TMJ conditions involve no invasions and are aimed at providing relief.

How long is the treatment?

Length of treatment is dependent on the severity of the condition. Relief for some patients comes in a matter of weeks, while other patients need to return for ongoing treatment to keep relief.

Do I need to have surgery?

The majority of TMJ disorders respond to conservative care. Surgery is infrequently needed and is generally reserved for difficult cases.
